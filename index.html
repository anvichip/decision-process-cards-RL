1AI Institute, University of South Carolina, USA
2Indian AI Research Organization (IAIRO), India

Abstract

Reinforcement Learning is now a central label in LLM post-training, reasoning, and agent research, yet reward gains alone do not identify the decision process being improved. RL means different things in different large language model (LLM) studies, and ambiguity makes it difficult to evaluate what a reported improvement actually demonstrates. The same terms can refer to fixed-data preference fitting, learned-reward optimization, verifiable-reward RL, inference-time search, tool-mediated control, or deployed policy adaptation, each supporting different scientific conclusions.

We argue that papers applying RL to LLMs should include Decision-Process Cards (DPC), a tiered, claim-level report of the state or belief, action granularity, reward and cost source, support assumptions, value or uncertainty object, model interface, and adaptation surface underlying the claim. The DPC is a claim-ceiling device, not a requirement that every paper address every RL challenge. Completion-only preference papers require different evidence than RLVR systems, tool-using agents, or deployed adaptive agents.

Our review suggests that the evidence does not support the claim that language-model RL is shallow. Instead, it points to substantial progress across multiple settings whose boundaries, assumptions, and evidentiary standards remain weakly standardized. Making DPC fields explicit would allow reviewers and readers to ask what was optimized, under what data support, with which reward or verifier validity, under which constraints, and across which adaptation surface.

The nine fields of a Decision-Process Card

Claim tier
Prevents one-shot response tuning from be- ing read as agentic control
State or belief
Separates completion MDPs from POMDP control and personalization
Action granularity and horizon
Exposes whether credit and return seman- tics are assigned at the correct time scale
Reward and cost
Makes proxy validity, constraint semantics, and reward hacking inspectable
Prompt/task support
Prevents broad prompt coverage from stand- ing in for response or trajectory coverage
Response/trajectory support
Converts offline and RLVR claims into au- ditable support claims
Value and uncertainty
Distinguishes diversity sampling from epis- temic exploration and calibrated risk
Model interface
Distinguishes search over text from model- based control
Adaptation surface
Separates context, memory mutation, weight updates, and rollback scopes

A DPC does not ask every paper to solve every RL problem — it asks each claim to state, in these nine terms, exactly which decision process it improved.
